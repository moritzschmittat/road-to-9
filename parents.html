For parents and teachers

Road to 9 helps a student plan GCSE revision: what to revise each day, and when to come back to it. It is their space, built to be calm and private.

What it does

  • The student rates every spec point of their subjects, from very weak to very strong.
  • Each day it suggests a short list of revision sessions, weakest topics first, within the time the student says they have.
  • Topics come back as short refreshers at growing gaps, the way memory research recommends, and past papers join in the spring.

What it doesn't do

  • No ads, and no selling or sharing of data.
  • No messaging, comments or public profiles, so strangers can't make contact through it.
  • No streaks, points or league tables, and no comparing students with each other.
  • No tracking cookies or analytics.

What we keep

An email address for sign-in links, an optional first name, and the planner itself: subjects, ratings, sessions, notes and exam dates. Nobody else can read a student's planner. We can see account details, like when someone last signed in, but never what is in their planner. Students can download or delete everything in Settings at any time, and accounts unused for 12 months are deleted after a warning email. The privacy note has the details, including how we checked the risks.

Age and consent

Road to 9 is for students aged 13 and over. When they join, students under 18 confirm that a parent or carer knows they are using it. There is no parent view: the plan belongs to the student, which helps them be honest about what they don't know yet.

Study groups

Students can make a small study group with friends using a code they share in person. Inside a group they can choose to show which topics they could help with, or that they are studying right now. All of this is off until they switch it on, and there is still no messaging.

See it for yourself

Take a test drive: it opens the planner of Sam, a made-up student, right in your browser. Nothing to sign up for, and nothing you do is saved.

Who we are
